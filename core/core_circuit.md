# 10-Minute Bodyweight Core Circuit

**Format:** 3 sets of 6 exercises | ~3 minutes per set | 30-45 seconds rest between sets

---

## Exercise Order & Timing

### 1. **Plank**
**Duration:** 45 seconds

**Form:**
- Start in forearm plank position (or full plank on hands if stronger)
- Body in a straight line from head to heels
- Engage your core — don't let hips sag or pike upward
- Breathe steadily; don't hold your breath
- Shoulders stacked over elbows

**Why first:** Establishes core tension and acts as a warm-up. Static hold primes the nervous system.

---

### 2. **Dead Bugs**
**Duration:** 45 seconds (alternating sides)

**Form:**
- Lie on your back, arms extended toward ceiling, knees bent at 90°
- Slowly extend opposite arm overhead and opposite leg outward
- Return to start with control
- Alternate sides in a slow, deliberate rhythm
- Keep lower back pressed to the floor throughout

**Why here:** Controlled, dynamic movement after the static plank. Low intensity allows recovery while keeping heart rate elevated.

---

### 3. **Bird Dogs**
**Duration:** 45 seconds (alternating sides)

**Form:**
- Start on hands and knees (tabletop position)
- Extend opposite arm straight ahead and opposite leg straight back
- Hold for 1-2 seconds, feeling the glute and core engagement
- Return with control and alternate sides
- Keep your hips level — don't rotate

**Why here:** Builds on the plank foundation. Introduces unilateral (single-side) work and adds glute activation.

---

### 4. **Mountain Climbers**
**Duration:** 45 seconds

**Form:**
- Start in a high plank position (hands under shoulders)
- Drive one knee toward your chest, then quickly alternate
- Keep your core tight and hips level
- Maintain a steady, controlled pace (not maximum speed)
- Chest stays over hands

**Why here:** Cardiovascular burst midway through the circuit. Elevates heart rate and adds dynamic core work.

---

### 5. **Glute Bridges**
**Duration:** 45 seconds

**Form:**
- Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat on the floor hip-width apart
- Drive through your heels to lift hips toward the ceiling
- Squeeze your glutes at the top
- Lower with control and repeat
- Keep chest up and avoid arching your lower back excessively

**Why here:** Active recovery after the intense mountain climbers. Shifts focus to posterior chain while maintaining core engagement.

---

### 6. **Side Plank** (or Pallof Press with band)
**Duration:** 30 seconds per side (60 seconds total)

**Form (Side Plank):**
- Lie on your side, prop yourself up on your forearm
- Body in a straight line from head to feet
- Stack your top foot on the bottom foot, or stagger them for more stability
- Press your forearm into the ground to lift hips
- Don't let hips sag toward the ground
- Repeat on the other side

**Form (Pallof Press with resistance band):**
- Stand perpendicular to an anchored band at chest height
- Hold the band at your chest with both hands
- Press the band straight out in front of you, resisting rotation
- Return to chest and repeat for desired reps
- Switch sides halfway through

**Why last:** Lateral core work. Targets obliques and anti-rotation stability. Finishes strong but allows for slightly lower intensity.

---

## Rest & Progression

**Between exercises:** 10-15 seconds transition time (shake out, reset position)

**Between sets:** 30-45 seconds active rest (walk, light stretching, catch your breath)

---

## Total Time Breakdown

- **Set 1:** ~3 minutes
- **Rest:** 30-45 seconds
- **Set 2:** ~3 minutes
- **Rest:** 30-45 seconds
- **Set 3:** ~3 minutes

**Total:** ~10-11 minutes

---

## Progression Tips

**Easier:** Reduce hold times or reps by 10-15 seconds per exercise

**Harder:**
- Increase hold times to 60+ seconds
- Add 1-2 second pauses at peak difficulty (plank, bird dog)
- Slow down reps for more time under tension (dead bugs, glute bridges)
- Add a fourth set
- Progress to harder variations (e.g., single-leg glute bridge, RKC plank)

---

## Notes

- This circuit balances **anti-flexion** (planks), **anti-rotation** (side planks), **dynamic stability** (bird dogs), **posterior chain** (glute bridges), and **cardiovascular demand** (mountain climbers)
- The order flows from static → dynamic → intense → active recovery → lateral stability
- Core work is most effective with **consistency** — 3-4 times per week is ideal
- Always prioritize **form over speed**
